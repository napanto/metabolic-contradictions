# Operational accounting amendment — 12 November 2026

One full-extraction request lost its connection without a response or generation ID. All further paid calls stopped automatically; completed extraction and benchmark work was checkpointed. OpenRouter's authenticated current-key accounting was queried twice after all other in-flight requests had settled. The snapshots, more than one minute apart, both exactly matched the sum of settled response costs; no additional charge was observed.

The incident was reconciled as **zero additional cost observed at those checks**, not as a fabricated per-call usage response. Its original usage remains null. The entire worst-case reservation remains charged against available project budget as a separate liability reserve, so a later charge within that bound cannot cause the USD40 cap to be exceeded. The response-backed spend and this conservative upper bound are distinguished. A final provider-usage audit is required after paid work ends.

This extends the original protocol's response-only accounting in an operational failure case. Stable usage snapshots do not guarantee that delayed billing is impossible; the retained reserve addresses that uncertainty. It changes neither the model-selection rule nor any scientific labels, prompts or evaluation splits. The incident, timestamps, source of reconciliation and reserve remain in `results/spend.json` and the two sanitized usage-snapshot files. No key or authorization header was saved.

Subsequent transport losses use the same reconciliation rule. A bounded supervisor may restart the unchanged classification stage only after its workers have drained, the sole unsettled reservations are identified transport failures, and two authenticated usage snapshots at least 60 seconds apart match the settled ledger exactly. Routing errors, cancellations, mixed/live pending requests, stale stage summaries, usage mismatches, and non-accounting failures require separate review. Each automatic reconciliation retains its snapshots and the full original worst-case reserve; it never invents per-generation usage or changes an output label. Ten automatic reconciliations per supervisor invocation are the operational limit.

HTTP pooling/concurrency and ledger serialization are operational settings. After frequent connection failures with disabled keepalive, bounded pooling was restored. Profiling also found repeated JSON encoding/decoding of the growing ledger to be a CPU bottleneck; pinned `orjson` now handles that file only, with semantic round-trip equality checked against the actual ledger. Cross-process locks, atomic replacement, reservations, settlement arithmetic, and canonical request/cache serialization are unchanged. Transport and cache-validation revisions are timestamped in `results/metabolic_classification_lock.json`.

Known-free HTTP429 rejections are now separated from model-output failures: at most 12 attempts use bounded backoff and a finite nonnegative Retry-After header; then the stage drains and the supervisor may perform a bounded cooldown. The invalid-output policy remains exactly one schema repair. Sanitized diagnostics identify the provider's shared upstream pool as the limit source. Eighty earlier, exclusively zero-cost rejected batches were archived for one operational retry, with every underlying call verified against the ledger (`results/classification_rate_recovery.json`). Their rejected rows are not classifier labels or semantic failures. No provider, prompt, candidate, decoding or schema choice changed.
